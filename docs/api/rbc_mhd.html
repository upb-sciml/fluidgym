Magnetohydrodynamic Rayleigh-Bénard Convection (RBC-MHD)

RBC-MHD models Rayleigh-Bénard convection of a liquid metal in a horizontal magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\). A box is heated from below and cooled from above, and the field points along \(x\), normal to the two Hartmann walls. The Lorentz force damps motion across the field lines. As a result, the flow organizes into convection rolls aligned with \(\mathbf{B}\), whose number and stability depend on how strong buoyancy is compared with the magnetic field [1].

The system is governed by three dimensionless parameters:

  • the Rayleigh number \(\mathrm{Ra}\), which sets the strength of buoyancy;

  • the Chandrasekhar number \(Q = \mathrm{Ha}^2\), which sets the strength of the magnetic field (\(\mathrm{Ha}\) is the Hartmann number);

  • the Prandtl number \(\mathrm{Pr}\), a material property of the fluid.

The Lorentz force relative to inertia is measured by the Stuart number \(N = Q \sqrt{\mathrm{Pr} / \mathrm{Ra}}\).

All environments use \(Q = 10^3\) (\(\mathrm{Ha} \approx 31.6\)), \(\mathrm{Pr} = 0.025\) (a liquid metal) and a box of \(5 \times 1 \times 5\) in units of the height \(H\). The grid is refined towards the Hartmann walls, so that the thin Hartmann layers are resolved by at least min_layer_cells cells.

The resolution is the number of cells along the vertical axis \(y\). The \(x\) and \(z\) axes have round(aspect_ratio * resolution) cells.

Reward

Unlike Rayleigh-Bénard Convection (RBC), the objective is to increase convective heat transfer, at a small cost in the electrical power needed for actuation. The volume-averaged Nusselt number is

\[\mathrm{Nu} = 1 + \sqrt{\mathrm{Ra} \, \mathrm{Pr}} \, \langle u_y \, (T - T_{\mathrm{ref}}) \rangle_V\]

where \(u_y\) is the vertical velocity, \(T\) the temperature, \(T_{\mathrm{ref}}\) the mean plate temperature, and \(\langle \cdot \rangle_V\) the volume average. The reward is

\[r_t = \frac{\mathrm{Nu}}{\mathrm{Nu}_{\mathrm{ref}}} - 1 - w_P \, \frac{P_{\mathrm{el}}}{P_{\mathrm{ref}}}\]

Here \(P_{\mathrm{el}}\) is the electrical power the electrodes feed into the fluid and \(w_P\) is power_penalty_weight (default 0.01). Both terms are averaged over the simulation steps of one environment step. \(\mathrm{Nu}_{\mathrm{ref}}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{ref}}\) are the mean Nusselt number and the mean buoyancy power of the uncontrolled flow. Joule heating in the Hartmann layers costs much more power than buoyancy provides, which is why \(w_P\) is small.

In MARL mode, each agent’s reward combines two parts. The first is local_reward_weight times the same Nusselt term, evaluated over the columns of the agent’s observation window. The second is the rest of the global reward, as in RBCEnv3D.

Action Space

The flow is controlled by electrodes in the two Hartmann walls. Each wall is divided into n_electrodes patches along \(y\) (default 2) and n_stations stations along \(z\). The default of n_stations is round(aspect_ratio * n_electrodes), which gives 10. Each patch holds an electrode with a raised-cosine profile. It covers electrode_fill (default 0.5) of the patch pitch in \(y\) and \(z\), and insulating wall surrounds it. Electrode \(k\) exists on both walls. The pair carries the current \(I_k\), half of it through each wall.

The actions in \([-1, 1]\) set the currents \(I_k\). They are:

  • centred to zero net current, which the insulating box requires,

  • clipped to the action bounds and centred again,

  • scaled by max_current (in units of \(\sigma U B H^2\)).

  • SARL (default): one action of shape (n_stations, n_electrodes), i.e. (10, 2).

  • MARL: each station along \(z\) is an agent that sets the currents of its column of electrodes, giving 10 agents with actions of shape (n_electrodes,).

Observation Space

Observations are a dictionary with the temperature and the three velocity components at a regular grid of sensors. There are 2 sensors per electrode station along \(z\) and 8 sensors along \(y\). The \(x\) axis gets the same sensor spacing as \(z\).

  • SARL: all sensors. The temperature has shape (20, 8, 20) and the velocity (3, 20, 8, 20) (ordered \(z, y, x\)).

  • MARL: each agent observes a window of local_obs_window (default 3) stations centred on its own, i.e. (6, 8, 20). Beyond the side walls, the window is padded with the quiescent conduction state.

Difficulty Levels

Difficulty is set by the Rayleigh number at fixed \(Q = 10^3\), so the Hartmann layers keep the same thickness:

Level

Rayleigh

Regime [1]

Easy

Ra = 2×10⁴ (20 Q)

Steady rolls

Medium

Ra = 5×10⁴ (50 Q)

Transition from 4 to 3 rolls

Hard

Ra = 1×10⁵ (100 Q)

Boundary of the no-roll regime

At higher Rayleigh numbers the flow is more chaotic, and the Lorentz force acting on it is weaker (lower \(N\)). The hard level uses a finer grid.
